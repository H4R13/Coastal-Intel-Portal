01 / Wildlife & habitats

Potential exposure

Feeding can transfer a persistent contaminant into aquatic organisms.

Potential impact

Species and chemical properties determine whether tissue concentrations affect survival or reproduction.

Evidence needed

Matched tissue samples, chemical analysis and trophic-position measurements.

02 / Fisheries & livelihoods

Potential exposure

Harvested fish may connect the food web to seafood markets.

Potential hardship

Confirmed contamination could create testing costs, loss of market confidence or income disruption.

Evidence needed

Edible-tissue testing, landing-site sampling and livelihood assessments.

03 / Seafood consumers

Potential exposure

Consumption is a possible route; dose depends on tissue concentration and intake.

Potential impact

Health effects require chemical-specific assessment. No illness or affected-person count is predicted here.

Evidence needed

Verified concentrations, intake patterns and suitable toxicological benchmarks.

Model, formulas & percentage meanings

All years, including 2020–2026, are modeled illustrations. No historical measurements, live feed or calibrated Indus Delta forecast are loaded. The graphic borrows a delta setting; it is not a GIS map or a verified local food web.

C₁(y) = 100 × (1 + g) ^ min(y − 2020, 6) × (1 + h) ^ max(y − 2026, 0)
g = selected annual source-trend fraction. h = g when continuing, 0 when stabilizing, or −0.02 when declining from 2027.

C(TL,y) = C₁(y) × TMF ^ (TL − 1)
Change(TL,y) % = 100 × [C(TL,y) / C(TL,2020) − 1]
Predator / phytoplankton ratio = TMF³

TMF is an assumed factor per trophic level, held constant through time. TMF > 1 illustrates magnification; TMF = 1 no increase between levels; TMF < 1 dilution. A field TMF is typically derived from a regression of log concentration against trophic position; this demo uses the corresponding idealized relationship and does not estimate a TMF from data. All concentrations need comparable tissue and chemical normalization in a real study.

The temporal percentage is identical at all trophic levels because the same source trend and fixed TMF apply to each level. Percentage curves therefore overlap. Rising concentrations across trophic levels and rising concentrations across years are different comparisons.

Pressure categories are arbitrary demo thresholds on temporal change: declining <0%; baseline 0–<25%; moderate 25–<75%; high 75–<150%; very high ≥150%. These are neither safety limits nor probabilities. A baseline category does not establish safety, and a high category does not establish toxicity.

This is a generic persistent-contaminant illustration, not a simulation of plastic particles or a contaminant-specific mercury, PCB or pesticide forecast. Water concentration, sediment exchange, uptake/elimination time, food-web complexity, storms and intervention delays are not modeled. Tissue values respond immediately to the input assumptions in this simplified model.

Source control changes the assumed trend; no real intervention effectiveness is inferred. Impact cards describe conditional pathways, not confirmed Indus Delta damage or human suffering.
